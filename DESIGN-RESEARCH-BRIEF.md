# Design research brief — ERA Sanitary & Plumbing Solutions

**For a research assistant (Gemini). Paste or upload this whole file.**

I have a live bilingual website for a sanitary and plumbing materials supplier in
Dhaka, Bangladesh. I want to improve its design. This brief tells you what the
business is, what the site already does, and — most importantly — **what you must
not propose**, because a large set of the usual recommendations are already ruled
out for reasons given below.

Read the "Do not propose" and "Already known" sections before answering. If an
idea falls in either, leave it out. I would rather have six usable
recommendations than thirty with twenty-four I have to discard.

---

## 1. What I want back, in this order

1. **A verdict on the visual direction.** The site is near-black with a gold
   accent. Is that right for a building-materials supplier selling to Bangladeshi
   contractors, or does the category expect something lighter and more
   utilitarian? This is my biggest open question — answer it with evidence about
   the category and the market, not taste.
2. **Conversion.** What actually moves a contractor, and separately a homeowner,
   to make contact on a supplier's site. Where the current structure (below)
   likely loses them.
3. **Concrete changes**, ranked by impact against effort, each one implementable
   within the constraints in section 4.

Cite sources where the claim is about the market rather than about design
principle. Say when you are unsure rather than filling the gap.

## 2. The business

- **ERA Sanitary & Plumbing Solutions**, Rampura, Dhaka. Trading from the same
  address since September 2003.
- **Two things at once:** it sells materials over a counter (C-PVC, U-PVC and
  pressure PVC-U pipe systems, bathroom fittings, ceramics, kitchen items) *and*
  takes on the fitting work itself across eleven trades — plumbing, tiles and
  marble, core cutting, deep tube wells, swimming pools, damp proofing, water
  proofing, electrical, painting, Thai glass, civil.
- **Two audiences, and this is the central design tension.** Contractors and
  developers buying wholesale, who care about stock depth, matching sizes, and
  delivery they can plan around. And homeowners buying a single tap, who need
  reassurance and advice. The same pages serve both.
- **Two languages**, English and Bengali, both equally real.
- Open seven days, 07:00–22:00. Delivery across 14 areas of Dhaka.
- Contact happens on **WhatsApp**. That is the primary conversion action on
  every page — not a form submission, not a phone call.

## 3. The site as it stands

**Structure.** 32 public pages: five flat pages (home, products, services, about,
contact) plus eleven trade pages, each existing in both languages on real,
crawlable URLs — English at the root, Bengali under `/bn/`. The language switch
is an ordinary link, not a JavaScript toggle, so both languages are indexable and
shareable.

**Home page order**, top to bottom:

1. Sticky top bar — retail/wholesale, opening hours, a live open-now badge
2. Header — logo, nav, language switch, WhatsApp button
3. Hero — headline, short copy, two actions, one image
4. Three pillars — quality products, reliable service, professional solutions
5. **What we supply** — the seven material categories as cards
6. **Why contractors keep coming back** — four reasons
7. A scrolling marquee of the 14 delivery areas
8. **Our services** — the eleven trades, each card linking to its own page and
   carrying a "Request a quote" button that deep-links to the form with that
   trade preselected
9. A call-to-action band — "send us your item list"
10. Footer

**Visual system** (fully documented in `DESIGN.md`, attached):

- Near-black grounds `#0b0b0d` / `#0e0f12`, raised surfaces `#141519`.
- A single accent: amber `#f7b500`. Blue and red exist only inside the logo.
- Display face Archivo, body face Barlow, with **Hind Siliguri listed after the
  Latin face in both stacks** so Bengali falls to the right face with no
  per-language CSS.
- Bengali never takes uppercase or letter-spacing — it has no case, and tracking
  breaks its conjuncts. Labels that are uppercase in English stay sentence case
  in Bengali.
- Bengali copy uses Bengali numerals (১২৩); `tel:` links stay in Latin digits so
  dialling works.
- 1200px shell, 8/12/18px radii, section rhythm `clamp(52px, 6.5vw, 92px)`.

**Weight.** Home page: 37 KB HTML, 33 KB CSS, 11 KB JavaScript. No framework. No
npm package ships to the browser. The whole site is generated by a single Node
script from two JSON files.

**The one third-party request on a public page** is the Google Fonts stylesheet
(`fonts.googleapis.com`, `fonts.gstatic.com`). Everything else is self-hosted.

## 4. Do not propose these

Each of these has already been decided and argued. Proposing them wastes your
answer.

1. **No invented facts of any kind.** No testimonials, no "500+ customers", no
   "10 years of excellence", no certifications, no awards, no brand logos, no
   prices. If it is not supplied by the business, it does not go on the site.
   This rules out most "add social proof" advice in its usual form — tell me what
   to do *instead*, given I have none of it yet.
2. **No third-party script on any customer-facing page.** No chat widget, no
   analytics, no review embed, no cookie banner, no tag manager, no A/B testing
   tool, no heatmap. A deferred script that stalls blocks every script behind
   it, and on Dhaka mobile data that is when it happens.
3. **Zero dependencies.** No React, no Tailwind, no jQuery, no component library,
   no build toolchain beyond the one Node script. Static HTML and CSS.
4. **It must work with JavaScript off.** Nothing essential may be JS-only.
5. **Contrast is measured, not estimated** — 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for non-text and
   UI boundaries. Any colour you propose must state its ratio against the ground
   it sits on.
6. **Both languages, always**, on real URLs. No JavaScript language toggle.
7. **No `aggregateRating` or `review` structured data.** Google's policy
   disallows self-serving review markup and names `LocalBusiness` as the case.
8. **No layout shift**; every image carries dimensions or an aspect ratio.
9. **No horizontal scroll at 390px.**
10. **The address, phone numbers, opening hours and map location are fixed.**

Free hosting, no server, no backend beyond a Firestore collection for quote
requests. Anything requiring a paid service or a running server is out.

## 5. Already known — do not spend research here

- **There are no photographs.** Every image slot renders a labelled placeholder;
  the home page alone has nine. The client has not sent them yet. I know this is

  What I *would* value: how to design a page that still persuades while the
  photography is pending.
- **There are no reviews yet.** The section is omitted entirely rather than
  showing an empty frame.
- **The custom domain is not switched over yet.**
- The site is otherwise complete and deployed.

## 6. What I actually want researched

Ranked. If you only answer three, answer the first three.

1. **Dark-and-gold, for this category, in this market.** Does a near-black site
   read as premium to a Dhaka contractor or developer, or does it read as
   unfamiliar and therefore risky? What do building-material suppliers and
   hardware businesses in Bangladesh, India and the Gulf actually look like, and
   what do their buyers expect? If the answer is that the category runs light and
   utilitarian, say so plainly — I will change it.
2. **Contractor versus homeowner on one site.** How comparable suppliers resolve
   serving a trade buyer and a retail buyer at once. Separate entry points,
   one page with two paths, or something else. What wins, and what the cost is.
3. **WhatsApp-first conversion.** The whole site drives to WhatsApp rather than a
   form. What patterns work in South Asia — button placement, wording, whether a
   pre-filled message helps or deters, what makes someone tap rather than leave.
4. **Trust without reviews or photographs.** What a 23-year-old business with a
   fixed address can show instead, within constraint 1. What signals actually
   reduce risk for a first-time buyer in this market.
5. **Bilingual English/Bengali design.** Switch placement and labelling
   conventions, whether a Bengali-speaking user expects to land in Bengali, and
   whether Hind Siliguri paired with Archivo/Barlow is a good choice or whether
   something better-matched exists.
6. **The Google Fonts question.** Two render-affecting third-party requests on
   every page, for a visitor on Dhaka mobile data. Is the typography worth it, or
   should the faces be self-hosted, subset, or dropped for a system stack? What
   is the real measured cost in this market?
